Insight Engine — How Every Decision Is Made
This page is the "show your work" section. Every recommendation on this platform is based on a chain of evidence — not a single number, and never a black box. The engine requires at least two independent signals to cross their danger thresholds before it escalates to a recommendation. This prevents false alarms and ensures every action is defensible to regulators, auditors, and investors.
Two-signal rule: A single bad metric triggers a "watch" status. Only when two or more metrics simultaneously breach their thresholds does the system escalate to "recommended action." This mimics how experienced portfolio managers think — one warning can be noise, two warnings are a pattern.
HTGC had 4 simultaneous signals: Health below floor, IRR below hurdle, IFRS 9 Stage 3 credit flag, and DSCR below minimum. Four concurrent red flags is the clearest exit signal the system can produce.
Liquidity produced zero signals: Not a single liquidity metric is in danger zone. The green status is earned — the system didn't default to green; it verified every metric and found nothing to flag.
How it works: No alert fires on a single metric alone. The engine waits until at least two criteria cross their thresholds simultaneously before escalating — reducing false alarms and ensuring every recommended action has multi-dimensional support.

Knowledge Base — The Dictionary Behind Every Number
Every metric on this platform comes from a specific mathematical formula. This page shows the formula, explains it in plain language, and describes what different values mean. No black boxes — every number is traceable to its source.
Why formulas matter to governance: When the system says "exit HTGC," that recommendation is the output of specific calculations — not a judgment call. The formula chain means the decision can be replicated, challenged, and defended. This satisfies fiduciary duty requirements.
FRAGILE vs ROBUST framework: Every holding is classified as Fragile (exit candidate), Watch (monitor), or Robust (eligible for reinvestment). This classification drives all portfolio actions automatically and consistently.

Settings — The Investment Policy Statement in Digital Form
The sliders and thresholds on this page are the digital version of your Investment Policy Statement (IPS) — the formal document that defines what this portfolio is allowed to do. When a threshold is breached, the system automatically flags it. Changing a threshold here changes what the system considers acceptable.
Drift Trigger ±5%: If any holding drifts more than 5 percentage points from its target, the system automatically flags it for rebalancing. This prevents "drift creep" where a portfolio silently becomes something very different from what was approved.
Single-Name Limit 5%: No single company or fund can exceed 5% of the portfolio without board approval. KKR is currently at an approved exception of 19%. Any new addition triggers an immediate alert.
Hurdle Rate 10%: Any holding earning below 10%/yr is automatically classified as underperforming and flagged for review. HTGC (6.1%) and ICLN (3.2%) both fail this test — hence their exit recommendations.
