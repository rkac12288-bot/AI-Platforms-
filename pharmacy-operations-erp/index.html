STATUS LABELS — WHAT EACH STOCK STATUS MEANS
Available The product is ready to sell
This product is on the shelf, in good condition, and can be sold to customers right now. The quantity shown is what you currently have in the store. No action needed unless the quantity is getting low.
When you see it: Most products will show this status during normal operations.
Quarantine Temporarily held — cannot be sold
Something about this batch needs to be checked before it can be put on the shelf. This could mean the product arrived damaged, has a quality issue, needs a price label update, or is awaiting supplier documentation. Do not sell quarantine items until the status changes to Available.
Common reasons: Damaged shipment · Missing barcode · Awaiting quality check · Recalled batch
In Transit On its way — not yet received
A purchase order has been placed and the supplier has shipped the goods, but they have not yet arrived at the store. You can see this quantity is "coming" but do not count it as available stock. The delivery time shown tells you how many days until it typically arrives.
What to do: No action needed. Watch the expected delivery date.
PRICING ALERTS — WHAT THEY MEAN FOR THE CASHIER
✓ Compliant Price is within the legal limit
The selling price on the register is at or below the Maximum Retail Price (MSRP) — the highest price the law allows this product to be sold for. Everything is fine. The cashier can process the sale normally.
✕ Violation Selling price is above the legal maximum — transaction blocked
The price configured at the cash register is higher than the law allows. If a customer tries to buy this product, the system will block the sale and alert the cashier. The cashier must lower the price to the MSRP cap before the transaction can go through. This protects the store from legal penalties.
Example: Product costs $10, selling price set to $52, but MSRP cap is $48. The $4 overcharge triggers this alert. Cashier must change price to $48.
🔒 Cashier Terminal Hard Lock — Why the register is frozen
When a non-compliant product is scanned at checkout, the register freezes and cannot process the sale. This is intentional — it prevents the store from accidentally overcharging a customer. A manager or authorized cashier must review the price, approve the correction, and update it to the legal maximum before the sale continues. Every override is logged with who did it and when.
Hard lock steps: 1. Cashier scans item → register locks
2. Alert appears on screen showing correct vs. current price
3. Manager enters override code → price updates to MSRP
4. Sale proceeds · Log entry created
STOCK ALERTS — REORDER AND THRESHOLDS
🔴 Critical Low Stock — You need to order this now
The quantity currently in the store has dropped below the minimum safety level (called the reorder point). This means if you keep selling at the current rate without ordering more, you will run out before the next delivery arrives. The system fires this alert automatically the moment the threshold is crossed — there is no delay.
How to read it: Current = 37 units · Reorder Point = 51 · Shortfall = 14 units · Delivery = 6 days
Translation: You need to order at least 14 more units immediately.
🟡 Near Threshold — Watchlist item, order soon
The product is still above the minimum level, but not by much — typically less than 50% above the reorder point. It has not triggered a critical alert yet, but if sales continue at the current pace, it will within days. These items are on the "watchlist" — keep an eye on them and prepare a purchase order.
Rule: current_stock is between reorder_point and 1.5× reorder_point
What is the Reorder Point?
The reorder point is the stock level at which you must place a new order. It is calculated to make sure new stock arrives before you run out, taking into account how fast the product sells and how long the supplier takes to deliver. It is set per product and can be updated in Settings.
Formula (behind the scenes): Reorder Point = Average Daily Sales × Lead Time in Days + Safety Buffer
EXPIRY AND CLEARANCE ALERTS
🏷️ Markdown Trigger — Product expiring soon, price reduced to break even
When a product has 30 days or fewer until its expiry date AND there are still units in stock, the system automatically reduces the selling price to the product's cost price (what you paid for it). This means you will not make a profit, but you will recover your original investment instead of throwing the product away. A "Quick Sale" sticker is printed at the terminal.
Example: You bought a shampoo for $15. Normal selling price is $22. 28 days before expiry with 89 units left → price drops to $15. You recover $1,335 instead of losing it all.
🔴 Critical Expiry — Expires in 7 days or less
This product will expire within one week. Immediate action is required. If the markdown has not been activated yet, do it now. Consider moving the items to a prominent display near the checkout. After the expiry date, these items cannot legally be sold and must be removed from the shelf and written off.
AUDIT AND CAPITAL LOSS
📉 Capital Loss — Money lost from your inventory
Capital loss is the total financial value of goods that left the store without generating revenue. This happens in two ways: theft (products stolen by customers or staff) and breakage (products damaged and unable to be sold). The system calculates this using the cost price — what the store paid — not the selling price.
Formula: Capital Loss = Stolen Units × Cost Price + Broken Units × Cost Price
🕵️ Theft Loss — Products taken without payment
This is the financial loss from items that disappeared from inventory without a corresponding sale. The nightly reconciliation compares what you should have (based on sales, deliveries, and previous counts) against what you actually have (physical count). The difference is the estimated theft quantity. Multiply by cost price = theft capital loss.
Nightly formula: Theft Qty = Yesterday's Closing Stock − Broken Today − Sold Today − Actual Physical Count
🗑️ Breakage Loss — Products damaged and unsellable
Items that were accidentally broken, spilled, or damaged to the point they cannot be sold. Staff must log broken items daily. This figure feeds into the nightly audit so the system can distinguish between theft and legitimate damage when calculating the reconciliation.
🔄 Nightly Reconciliation — What runs at midnight
Every night at 23:59, the system automatically calculates the daily inventory movement. It compares the opening stock against all recorded activity (sales, deliveries, write-offs) and the physical count to identify any unexplained discrepancies. The result is surfaced on the manager's dashboard each morning as the daily capital loss report.
TERMS GLOSSARY
MSRP (Maximum Retail Price) — The legal price ceiling
The highest price at which a product may legally be sold to a customer. Set by the manufacturer or regulatory authority. You can sell below this price but never above it. Selling above the MSRP is a legal violation and triggers the cashier hard-lock.
Cost Price — What you paid the supplier
The price the store paid when it purchased the product from the supplier. This is the baseline for calculating profit margin, markdown prices, and capital loss. It is never shown to customers.
Gross Margin formula: Margin % = (Sell Price − Cost Price) / Sell Price × 100
Sell Price — What customers pay
The price displayed on the shelf and charged at the register. Must always be less than or equal to the MSRP. Can be changed by authorized staff. During a markdown event, it is automatically dropped to the cost price.
Days to Expire (DTE) — Time left before the product expires
Calculated daily as the number of days between today and the product's batch expiry date. When this reaches 30, the markdown trigger activates. When it reaches 0 or goes negative, the product has expired and must be removed from the shelf immediately.
