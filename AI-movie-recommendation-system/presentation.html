Why simple recommenders fail

Three shortcuts that quietly ruin recommendations.

Genre

Same label, different film

Matching on the genre name alone treats every “drama” as alike.

±1

Fake taste matches

With only two shared ratings, any two viewers look like perfect twins or perfect opposites.

6%

Missing quality data

Movies with no critics’ score get a flat average, and good films are buried.

The engine

Five ways to find the next film, and a story.

Content-based

Unwatched films ranked by genre match, IMDB rating, critics, popularity and recency.

Since you liked X

Nearest neighbours to the viewer’s favourite film by cosine similarity.

Collaborative

What viewers with correlated taste rated highly, by Pearson correlation.

Taste tribes

k-means groups viewers by genre preference and surfaces each group’s favourites.

Trending fallback

Popularity and critics’ score for brand-new viewers with no history.

Story dashboard

Five chapters that end in the recommendation, with a headline written from the data.

The insight

Statistical honesty is what makes a pick trustworthy.

4+

shared ratings before two viewers count as a taste match. With only two, the correlation is always ±1, a mathematical artifact.

Missing critics’ scores are predicted from each film’s IMDB rating (r = 0.63 across about 936 films), not filled with an average.

Group favourites are ranked by how many members agree, so one lucky 5-star never beats a film three people rated 4.
